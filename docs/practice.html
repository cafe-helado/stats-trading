Practice · No. 14

The questions, without the chapter headings

Thirteen modules of worked examples teach you the ideas. What they cannot do is ask you a question without telling you which idea it needs — and choosing the right tool is most of the difficulty. This page draws from all of them at once, remembers what you miss, and brings it back.

Scroll ↓

Start here

This is not a fourteenth lesson. It is the drill floor for the other thirteen, and it works differently from the practice at the foot of each module in three specific ways.

  1. It interleaves. A question arrives with no module attached, so you have to recognize what kind of problem it is before you can start. In a per-page drill you already know — that context is doing work you will not have when it matters.
  2. It spaces. Anything you get wrong comes back after a few questions, then after a dozen, then after fifty. Getting it right once moves it further away; missing it again drags it back to the front.
  3. It remembers. Your record is kept in this browser, so the weak-spot grid in chapter 02 is built from every session rather than the last five minutes.

There are 47 question types across the thirteen modules. Nothing here is new material — every formula was derived somewhere else in the series, and every answer links back to where.

01

Interleaved practice

One question at a time, drawn from any of the thirteen. The first job is working out what it is asking.

Answer in the box. Numbers only — units are shown beside it, and percentages want the number rather than the sign. Anything within the stated tolerance counts, so round sensibly rather than chasing decimals.

Every question tells you afterwards which module it came from and links there. Use that: if a topic keeps catching you, the fix is usually to reread one chapter, not to grind more questions at it.

What you are actually weak at

Accuracy per module, across every session. The grid is usually a surprise.

The reason to track this rather than trust a feeling is that difficulty and felt difficulty come apart badly. Topics that were hard to read often stick, and topics that felt obvious on the page are the ones that evaporate.

Bars are colored by accuracy, and grey means not enough attempts to say anything yet — under five, the number is noise, which is module 08 applied to your own record. The "weakest four" button in chapter 01 filters practice down to the four modules with the worst rate among those you have actually attempted.

03

Spacing, and the boxes

Each of the 47 question types sits in one of five boxes. Getting it right promotes it; missing it sends it back to the first.

The scheduler is a Leitner system, which is the simplest honest form of spaced repetition. Every question type carries a box number from 1 to 5, and the box sets how long before it can be asked again — measured in questions answered, not in time.

The intervals are 1, 3, 8, 21 and 55. Answer something correctly and it moves up a box, so it returns less often. Miss it and it drops straight back to box 1 regardless of how high it had climbed, which puts it in the group served first. Early on that group is most of the bank, so a missed item takes a little while to come round; once the rest have been promoted it returns almost immediately.

The effect is that practice concentrates itself. Early on almost everything is due and the mix is broad; as things settle, most of what you see is the handful you keep missing, and the material you have secured only reappears occasionally to check it is still there.

When nothing is due — which happens once most types have climbed — the page picks the item due soonest rather than stalling. So it never runs out, it just gets progressively less repetitive.

box 1 → 3 → 8 → 21 → 55 questions between repeats; a miss returns to 1

Reads as: things you know come back rarely, things you do not come back immediately. The intervals grow roughly threefold because forgetting is roughly exponential, so equal-ratio spacing keeps the difficulty of each recall about constant.

04

Exam

Twenty questions, no feedback until the end. Practice tells you how it is going; this tells you where you are.

The drill above gives an answer after every question, which is right for learning and wrong for measuring. Immediate feedback lets you correct course mid-session, and that makes the final score an overestimate of what you could do cold.

So: twenty questions, drawn evenly across the modules you have selected, marked only at the end. No box updates during it and no hints. It does count toward your record, because it is the most honest data the page collects — but it does not advance the scheduler's clock, since a measurement should not move the schedule it is measuring.

A score below about twelve usually means one or two modules are dragging rather than a general weakness — the breakdown at the end says which, and chapter 02's grid will confirm it after a few runs.

05

Why the page is shaped like this

Three design choices, and the honest limits of each.

Each of the three differences from a per-page drill is doing a specific job, and it is worth being clear about what they do and do not buy.

Interleaving exists because selecting the method is a separate skill from executing it, and a drill sitting under the chapter that taught it never exercises the first one. That is also why it feels worse: blocked practice produces a higher score during the session and interleaved practice produces a lower one. If this page feels harder than the drill at the foot of module 09, that is the mechanism working rather than failing.

Spacing exists because a question answered correctly three seconds after reading the answer has tested almost nothing. Putting distance between repeats forces an actual recall rather than a read-off, and the Leitner boxes are simply the cheapest way to arrange that without a clock.

Tracking exists because self-assessment is unreliable in a specific direction: fluency while reading gets mistaken for retrieval. The grid in chapter 02 is deliberately built from attempts rather than from how confident anything felt.

And the limits. Forty-seven question types is enough to stop you memorizing answers, since the numbers are regenerated every time, but it is not enough to cover everything the thirteen modules contain — the conceptual multiple-choice questions on each page test things a numeric drill structurally cannot. Nor does a numeric answer prove understanding: you can execute a Kelly formula perfectly and still size a real position badly, which is the whole argument of module 13's last chapter. Treat a full green grid as a floor rather than a finish.

Check yourself

You score worse on this page than on the drill at the foot of each module. What does that tell you?

The gap is expected and is roughly the point. The questions are drawn from the same formulas; what changed is that nothing tells you which one applies. A score that falls when the module label is removed is a precise measurement of how much of your performance was riding on that label.

Your record lives in this browser's local storage under a single key and is never transmitted; clearing site data, using a private window, or opening the series on another device all start you from nothing, and the erase button in chapter 02 does the same deliberately. The Leitner intervals are measured in questions answered rather than elapsed time, which is a simplification — real spaced repetition schedules against the clock, and a system that cannot tell a five-minute gap from a five-day one is approximating. The 1, 3, 8, 21, 55 spacing is a convention rather than a derivation. Accuracy per module is only meaningful once a module has been attempted a reasonable number of times, which is why the grid greys out anything under five attempts rather than reporting a confident-looking rate off two. And a numeric drill can only test what reduces to a number: the conceptual questions at the foot of each module cover ground this page structurally cannot reach, so a full grid here means the arithmetic is secure, not that the subject is.
